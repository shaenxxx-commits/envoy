# Lead Synthesis — Filter Assessment

## FACT — Run-Level

- Six filter questions assessed against Runs 001-016.
- Gates distinguished candidates partially.
- Numeric scores gave weak discrimination.
- UNKNOWN remained usable throughout.
- Research/Resource separation preserved at the filter
  level but Resource Fit was never exercised.
- Ability to Test (technical vs methodological) was
  the most decisive filter axis.
- Two ambiguities appeared: missing "observation cost",
  and single-valued "Existing Synthesis".

## OBSERVATION

- The filter worked best as a descriptive schema for
  comparing candidates, less as a scoring system.
- The decisive selection criteria (cluster size,
  maturity preference) came from the Architect, not
  from the filter.
- The filter's distinction between technical and
  methodological accessibility has proven durable
  across eleven field runs.

## UNKNOWN

- Whether the filter would perform similarly on a
  second selection exercise.
- Whether the two candidate amendments (A1, A2) are
  sufficient or whether further issues would appear
  in a new field.
- Whether Resource Fit can be measured at all in
  the current configuration.

## LEAD INTERPRETATION

The filter is not refuted by its first application.
It is confirmed as useful, with two known gaps:

1. Cost is a first-class variable that the filter
   does not encode.
2. Synthesis is not one-dimensional.

Neither gap invalidates the filter. Both are
recorded for the next revision, but no revision
is made now.

This is consistent with FIELD-SELECTION §8:
"Do not keep adding criteria before applying it."

## Decision

Filter retained for future selection.
Amendments A1-A3 recorded as candidates.
FIELD-SELECTION-v0.1 unchanged.
