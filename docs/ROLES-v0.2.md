## 1. Purpose

This document defines the separation of roles inside
Envoy.

The purpose is to prevent the Lead from silently
becoming the sole source of observation,
interpretation, and decision-making.

The roles are complementary but not interchangeable.

## 2. Architect

The Architect is the project owner.

The Architect:

- defines the existence and overall direction of Envoy;
- approves major changes in scope or method;
- decides whether the project continues, changes
  direction, or stops;
- may introduce new constraints or objectives;
- retains final responsibility for strategic
  decisions.

The Architect does not need to perform every
analytical operation personally.

The Architect may challenge the Lead, reject
conclusions, request additional verification,
or require a new reconnaissance cycle.

## 3. Lead

The Lead is responsible for operating the Envoy
method.

The Lead:

- prepares and structures reconnaissance tasks;
- maintains project state;
- preserves provenance;
- separates facts, observations, hypotheses,
  and unknowns;
- coordinates External nodes;
- compares independent outputs;
- identifies contradictions and methodological
  problems;
- proposes next actions;
- records results and changes to the method.

The Lead must not silently convert an interpretation
into a fact.

The Lead must distinguish:

LEAD OBSERVATION
LEAD INTERPRETATION
LEAD HYPOTHESIS
LEAD RECOMMENDATION

The Lead does not have unilateral authority to
redefine the project.

## 4. External

An External is an independent analytical node.

An External:

- examines supplied material;
- produces an independent assessment;
- identifies structures, alternatives,
  uncertainties, and methodological problems;
- may challenge the Lead or another External;
- may participate in controlled interaction when
  explicitly instructed.

An External does not own Envoy.

An External does not decide the project's final
direction.

An External must not claim access to information
it was not given.

## 5. Separation of authority and evidence

The roles have different functions:

ARCHITECT
-> strategic authority

LEAD
-> operational coordination and synthesis

EXTERNAL
-> independent analytical input

Authority does not determine factual correctness.

An Architect statement is not automatically a fact.

A Lead interpretation is not automatically a fact.

An External statement is not automatically a fact.

All claims remain subject to their own evidence
and provenance.

## 6. Independent evidence

External outputs are valuable partly because they
provide observations that were not generated by
the Lead's immediate reasoning.

Therefore the Lead should not pre-shape independent
External responses unnecessarily.

During an independent phase:

- each External receives the defined input;
- each External works without seeing the others;
- outputs are preserved before comparison.

The Lead must not remove disagreement merely to
produce a cleaner synthesis.

## 7. Interaction

When External outputs are introduced into another
External session, this is an interaction phase.

The Lead must record:

- which External received which material;
- in what order;
- what the input contained;
- what stage of the reconnaissance this represented.

Interaction-derived observations must remain
distinguishable from independent observations.

## 8. Decision flow

The normal decision flow is:

ARCHITECT
->
TASK / CONSTRAINT
->
LEAD
->
INDEPENDENT EXTERNALS
->
COMPARISON
->
LEAD SYNTHESIS
->
ARCHITECT REVIEW
->
NEXT ACTION

This is a working flow, not a claim that every
Envoy operation must use every role.

## 9. Conflict

When roles disagree:

### Architect vs Lead

The Architect has final strategic authority.

The disagreement and its basis should nevertheless
be recorded when relevant.

### Lead vs External

The Lead must not suppress the External disagreement
merely because it conflicts with the current Lead
interpretation.

The disagreement should be examined as evidence,
uncertainty, or a methodological issue.

### External vs External

Disagreement is preserved.

It may become a reason for:

- further verification;
- a controlled interaction;
- a new test;
- classification as unresolved.

Agreement is not required for a useful
reconnaissance result.

## 10. Unknown

Any role may explicitly declare:

UNKNOWN
UNCERTAIN
INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE
CANNOT VERIFY

Unknown is a legitimate project state.

No role should fill an information gap merely to
maintain narrative continuity.

## 11. Provenance

Every substantive project claim should be
attributable, where applicable, to its origin:

SOURCE
FIELD OBSERVATION
EXTERNAL
LEAD
ARCHITECT
RESULT

A later synthesis must not erase the distinction
between these origins.

## 12. Role boundaries

The following substitutions are prohibited:

Architect != Lead
Lead != External
External != Architect

In particular:

- the Lead must not present its synthesis as an
  External finding;
- an External must not be presented as the project
  decision-maker;
- the Architect's strategic preference must not be
  presented as empirical evidence.

## 13. Handoff

The role of Lead may be transferred between
LLM-sessions. The transfer protocol is defined in
meta/handoff-protocol.md.

The handoff itself does not change the role
definitions in this document.

## 14. First-cycle observations (Runs 001-017)

The following observations are recorded from the
first application of the role structure.

### 14.1 Architect

The Architect supplied two field-selection criteria
that were not present in the filter:

- cluster size (preference for smaller fields);
- developmental state (preference for emerging
  fields over saturated ones).

These criteria were decisive. The filter ranked
candidates, but the Architect made the selection.

This is consistent with §2: strategic authority
resides with the Architect.

Recorded as observation, not as a filter defect.
See Run 017 §A1 (candidate amendment).

### 14.2 Lead

The Lead performed operational coordination across
17 runs without intervening in the Architect's
strategic decisions.

The Lead authored two amendments to earlier
conclusions (Runs 013, 014) after external review
revealed an error. The amendments were recorded as
revision notices, not retroactive rewrites.

This is consistent with §3 and with
meta/handoff-protocol.md §13.

### 14.3 External

External nodes were used in four modes:

1. Independent reconnaissance (Runs 001-004).
2. Adversarial review of Lead work (Run 015).
3. Baseline verification (Runs 003, 004).
4. Archive recovery (Runs 001-002).

The adversarial review in Run 015 provided the
strongest case for the role design: two externals
(Kimi, Namazu) agreed with a Lead overclaim; one
external (Grok) contradicted it with an external
source; the contradiction was correct.

This is consistent with §6 and §9.

### 14.4 External archive behaviour

External persistent chats preserved enough context
to recover archived responses for Runs 001-002
after the original raw outputs were lost.

This property is documented in EXTERNALS-v0.2 §4.1
and was used in practice.

### 14.5 No interaction phase

The controlled interaction phase defined in §7
was not exercised in the first cycle.

This is recorded as an untested mechanism.

## 15. Status

STATUS: CONFIRMED.

The role structure completed one full field cycle
(Runs 001-017) without role substitution incidents.

Two aspects remain untested:

- controlled interaction between External nodes;
- role behaviour during a field stop or mission
  termination.

These are noted for future cycles.
