# ENVOY — DDA v0.1

## 1. Purpose

DDA is the working environment of Envoy for quiet field reconnaissance.

Envoy is deployed into a concrete field in order to:

- observe the real environment;
- identify its structure, needs, constraints, and opportunities;
- test methods under real conditions;
- discover practical niches;
- preserve field experience;
- where justified, turn a discovered niche into a source of resources for the main projects.

DDA is not an end in itself and does not prescribe the result in advance.

## 2. Core principle

First field, then model.

Envoy must not force reality into a predefined hypothesis.

Observation, action, result, and interpretation must remain distinguishable.

## 4. Two contours

### Research contour

Field → observation → data → hypothesis → test → knowledge.

### Resource contour

Field → need → opportunity → action → result → potential resource.

The contours may interact but must not be conflated.

A research result is not automatically a product.

A commercial opportunity is not automatically a research finding.

## 5. Knowledge states

FACT
OBSERVATION
HYPOTHESIS
UNKNOWN

FACT:
Confirmed by a source or directly established through a documented observation.

OBSERVATION:
Something Envoy encountered or recorded in the field that has not yet been elevated to a higher-level fact.

HYPOTHESIS:
An explanatory proposition requiring verification.

UNKNOWN:
Something Envoy does not currently know.

Forbidden automatic transitions:

Observation → Fact
Hypothesis → Fact
Unknown → Assumption

## 7. Quiet presence

Envoy does not need to expose its complete architecture, objectives, or research program to the field.

Operational priority:

observe before declare
understand before scale
test before conclude

Publicity, if it appears, is a consequence of field work rather than a prerequisite for it.

## 8. Relationship to other projects

Envoy is independent.

It may:

- use knowledge and methods from other projects;
- transmit verified results;
- generate new research questions;
- generate resources for further work.

However, provenance must remain separate.

LAB fact
!= Corpus representation
!= Envoy observation
!= Envoy interpretation
!= Envoy result

Shared sources do not imply shared project identity.

## 9. Initial success condition

The first phase does not need to prove the effectiveness of the whole Envoy system.

It is sufficient to establish that:

1. the field can actually be investigated;
2. the method produces distinguishable and useful observations;
3. at least some hypotheses can be tested in reality;
4. practical points of application can be identified;
5. field work produces cumulative knowledge.

A niche is not considered discovered merely because it appears promising.

## 10. Memory of the expedition

DDA preserves not only conclusions but the path by which they were reached.

WHAT WAS SEEN
↓
WHAT WAS THOUGHT
↓
WHAT WAS DONE
↓
WHAT HAPPENED
↓
WHAT CHANGED
↓
WHAT REMAINS UNKNOWN

This allows Envoy to learn from field experience rather than only from final conclusions.

## 11. Initial position

Envoy does not know in advance:

- which niche will be found;
- whether it will be commercial;
- which method will work best;
- which external nodes will prove useful;
- whether the method will need modification;
- whether reconnaissance will produce resources.

This uncertainty is part of the initial state of the expedition.

DDA records the path from unknown to tested knowledge.
