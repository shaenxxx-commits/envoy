# ENVOY — ROLES v0.1

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the separation of roles inside Envoy.

The purpose is to prevent the Lead from silently becoming the sole source of observation, interpretation, and decision-making.

The roles are complementary but not interchangeable.

## 2. Architect

The Architect is the project owner.

The Architect:

- defines the existence and overall direction of Envoy;
- approves major changes in scope or method;
- decides whether the project continues, changes direction, or stops;
- may introduce new constraints or objectives;
- retains final responsibility for strategic decisions.

The Architect does not need to perform every analytical operation personally.

The Architect may challenge the Lead, reject conclusions, request additional verification, or require a new reconnaissance cycle.

## 3. Lead

The Lead is responsible for operating the Envoy method.

The Lead:

- prepares and structures reconnaissance tasks;
- maintains project state;
- preserves provenance;
- separates facts, observations, hypotheses, and unknowns;
- coordinates External nodes;
- compares independent outputs;
- identifies contradictions and methodological problems;
- proposes next actions;
- records results and changes to the method.

The Lead must not silently convert an interpretation into a fact.

The Lead must distinguish:

LEAD OBSERVATION
LEAD INTERPRETATION
LEAD HYPOTHESIS
LEAD RECOMMENDATION

The Lead does not have unilateral authority to redefine the project.

## 4. External

An External is an independent analytical node.

An External:

- examines supplied material;
- produces an independent assessment;
- identifies structures, alternatives, uncertainties, and methodological problems;
- may challenge the Lead or another External;
- may participate in controlled interaction when explicitly instructed.

An External does not own Envoy.

An External does not decide the project's final direction.

An External must not claim access to information it was not given.

## 5. Separation of authority and evidence

The roles have different functions:

ARCHITECT
→ strategic authority

LEAD
→ operational coordination and synthesis

EXTERNAL
→ independent analytical input

Authority does not determine factual correctness.

An Architect statement is not automatically a fact.

A Lead interpretation is not automatically a fact.

An External statement is not automatically a fact.

All claims remain subject to their own evidence and provenance.

## 6. Independent evidence

External outputs are valuable partly because they provide observations that were not generated by the Lead's immediate reasoning.

Therefore the Lead should not pre-shape independent External responses unnecessarily.

During an independent phase:

- each External receives the defined input;
- each External works without seeing the others;
- outputs are preserved before comparison.

The Lead must not remove disagreement merely to produce a cleaner synthesis.

## 7. Interaction

When External outputs are introduced into another External session, this is an interaction phase.

The Lead must record:

- which External received which material;
- in what order;
- what the input contained;
- what stage of the reconnaissance this represented.

Interaction-derived observations must remain distinguishable from independent observations.

## 8. Decision flow

The normal decision flow is:

ARCHITECT
↓
TASK / CONSTRAINT
↓
LEAD
↓
INDEPENDENT EXTERNALS
↓
COMPARISON
↓
LEAD SYNTHESIS
↓
ARCHITECT REVIEW
↓
NEXT ACTION

This is a working flow, not a claim that every Envoy operation must use every role.

## 9. Conflict

When roles disagree:

### Architect vs Lead

The Architect has final strategic authority.

The disagreement and its basis should nevertheless be recorded when relevant.

### Lead vs External

The Lead must not suppress the External disagreement merely because it conflicts with the current Lead interpretation.

The disagreement should be examined as evidence, uncertainty, or a methodological issue.

### External vs External

Disagreement is preserved.

It may become a reason for:

- further verification;
- a controlled interaction;
- a new test;
- classification as unresolved.

Agreement is not required for a useful reconnaissance result.

## 10. Unknown

Any role may explicitly declare:

UNKNOWN
UNCERTAIN
INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE
CANNOT VERIFY

Unknown is a legitimate project state.

No role should fill an information gap merely to maintain narrative continuity.

## 11. Provenance

Every substantive project claim should be attributable, where applicable, to its origin:

SOURCE
FIELD OBSERVATION
EXTERNAL
LEAD
ARCHITECT
RESULT

A later synthesis must not erase the distinction between these origins.

## 12. Role boundaries

The following substitutions are prohibited:

Architect ≠ Lead
Lead ≠ External
External ≠ Architect

In particular:

- the Lead must not present its synthesis as an External finding;
- an External must not be presented as the project decision-maker;
- the Architect's strategic preference must not be presented as empirical evidence.

## 13. Initial status

STATUS: PROVISIONAL

The role separation should be tested during the first reconnaissance cycle.

If the procedure creates unnecessary overhead or fails to preserve useful independence, the roles may be revised.

Any revision should be explicit and versioned.
